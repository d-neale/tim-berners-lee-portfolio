1955

Birth

Tim Berners-Lee was born on June 8, 1955, in London, England, into a family of computer scientists. This laid the foundation for his later career

1980

Proposal for the World Wide Web

In 1980, while working at CERN, Berners-Lee proposed the idea of an information management system that would eventually become the World Wide Web (WWW). This early concept was fundamental to the web’s development

1989

Development of the World Wide Web

In March 1989, Berners-Lee, working at CERN, submitted a proposal that would evolve into the World Wide Web. By 1990, he had developed the first website, browser, and web server

1991

First Website Launched

The first website, "info.cern.ch," went live in 1991, making the World Wide Web accessible to the public for the first time

1994

Establishment of the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C)

Berners-Lee founded the W3C at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in 1994 to ensure that the web would develop in a standardized and open way

2004

Knighthood and Continued Advocacy

In 2004, Berners-Lee was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II for his contributions to the development of the web. He has since been an advocate for web standards, privacy, and open data
